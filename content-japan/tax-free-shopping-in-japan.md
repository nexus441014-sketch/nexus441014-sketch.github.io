## What changed

Until recently, eligible visitors had consumption tax removed at the register. You showed
your passport, paid the lower price, and that was the end of it.

**From 1 November 2026, Japan moved to a refund-at-departure model.** You pay the full
price including tax in the shop, and claim the tax back after a customs check when you
leave the country. The stated reason is to stop goods bought tax-free being resold inside
Japan: the refund is released once the goods are confirmed to be leaving.

Purchases made before the switchover still follow the old rules. Anything bought now does
not.

> This is a live change, and the practical details are still settling. Treat everything
> below as the shape of the system, and confirm the current procedure on the official
> sources at the bottom of this page before you rely on it.

## What this means in practice

**You need more money on the day.** This is the part that catches people. If you were
budgeting around the tax-excluded price, your actual spend at the register is now higher,
and the difference comes back later. On a large purchase that gap is real. Factor it into
[how much cash and credit you carry](/articles/cash-or-card-in-japan.html).

**There is now a step at the airport you cannot skip.** The refund depends on clearing a
check before you leave. Miss it and you do not get the money.

**Timing matters.** There is a limit on how long after purchase you can depart and still
claim. Buying on an early stop of a long trip is not automatically safe.

## The departure step

The check happens in the international departure area, and the important detail is the
order of operations: **do it before you check your bags in**, because the goods may need
to be available for inspection.

At the major international airports you can prepare through
[Visit Japan Web](https://services.digital.go.jp/en/visit-japan-web/), the government
service that also handles immigration and customs declarations. Setting that up before
you fly is worth the ten minutes.

Expect queues. A new procedure plus everyone's flight departing at the same hours is a
predictable combination, and the early months of any such change are the worst. Build
extra time into your airport arrival, especially on a tight connection.

## How you actually get the money

The refund is paid by the shop or the refund operator it uses, not by customs. The method
depends on where you bought the item and can include a card refund, a bank transfer, an
app, or cash at the airport.

Because it varies, **ask at the register when you buy**, not at the airport when you
leave. The questions worth asking are: how the refund reaches me, how long it takes, and
whether any handling fee is deducted.

## Keep the goods as sold

The system is built around goods leaving the country unused. Practically: keep the
packaging, keep the receipt and paperwork together, and do not unpack or use consumables
you intend to claim on. If you want to use something during the trip, buy it normally and
forget the refund.

This interacts awkwardly with sending your shopping ahead by
[luggage forwarding](/articles/luggage-forwarding-in-japan.html). If goods are not with
you at departure, you may not be able to present them. Think about that before you ship a
suitcase to the airport.

## Should you bother?

For a few small souvenirs: probably not. The paperwork, the queue and the risk of missing
the step outweigh the amount involved.

For a genuinely large purchase — electronics, a watch, a significant clothing haul — the
sum is worth the procedure. Plan for it rather than discovering it at the counter.

## Official sources

Because this change is recent and still bedding in, go to the authorities rather than to
travel blogs, including this one:

- [National Tax Agency](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/) — the tax authority
- [Japan Customs](https://www.customs.go.jp/english/) — the departure-side procedure
- [Visit Japan Web](https://services.digital.go.jp/en/visit-japan-web/) — the official
  service for the airport steps
- [Japan National Tourism Organization](https://www.japan.travel/en/) — visitor-facing
  summaries
