## First, which airport

**Haneda** is close to central Tokyo. **Narita** is a long way out, in a different
prefecture. If you have a choice when booking, this difference is worth real money and
about an hour of your life in each direction.

If you have already booked, the decision below is about which service from your airport.

## The four factors

1. **What time do you land?** Late arrivals lose options fast. Rail and bus services stop
   earlier than visitors expect, and the last connection from Narita is not late.
2. **How much luggage?** A large case changes the calculation towards a bus or a taxi,
   because they are door-to-door and you are not lifting anything onto a train.
3. **Where are you staying?** A hotel near a major station favours the train. Somewhere
   residential favours the bus or a taxi for the last stretch.
4. **How many of you?** For three or four people, a taxi stops looking extravagant
   compared with four separate train fares, particularly late at night.

## The options in plain terms

**Express trains** are the fastest from Narita and reliable from both airports. They run
on a fixed schedule, with reserved seating on some services and luggage space designed
for airport use. The weakness is that they deliver you to a major station, not to your
hotel, so you still have a last leg with your bags.

**Local and metro lines** are the cheapest. They are also the most tiring: more changes,
more stairs, and carriages that are not built for suitcases. Reasonable from Haneda,
punishing from Narita after a long flight.

**Airport buses** run to major hotels and districts. Slower in traffic, but you sit down,
your luggage goes in the hold, and many routes stop at or near hotels. For a first
arrival with a big case, this is frequently the right answer even though nobody
recommends it online.

**Taxis** are expensive from Narita and reasonable from Haneda, especially shared between
several people or very late at night when little else is running.

## Set these up before you leave the airport

- **Get online.** Sort connectivity before you walk away from the arrivals hall. See
  [eSIM, pocket WiFi or SIM](/articles/esim-pocket-wifi-or-sim-japan.html).
- **Get an IC card.** It makes every subsequent journey trivial. See
  [IC cards explained](/articles/ic-cards-suica-pasmo-explained.html).
- **Consider sending the suitcase.** You can forward it to your hotel and travel in with
  a day bag. See [luggage forwarding](/articles/luggage-forwarding-in-japan.html).
- **Have your hotel's address in Japanese** saved on your phone, and the phone number.
  This matters for taxis and for luggage forwarding.

## The late arrival problem

If you land in the evening, check the last departure for your intended service *before*
you fly, not while standing in the arrivals hall. Options thin out quickly, and the
fallback from Narita is an expensive taxi or an airport hotel.

Both airports publish current access information and timetables:

- [Narita Airport](https://www.narita-airport.jp/en/) — official access guide
- [Haneda Airport](https://tokyo-haneda.com/en/) — official access guide

## The default recommendations

- **Haneda, any time, light luggage:** train. It is quick and simple.
- **Haneda, late or heavy:** taxi, especially shared.
- **Narita, daytime, light luggage:** express train.
- **Narita, heavy luggage or a hotel away from a main station:** airport bus.
- **Narita, late arrival:** check the last services before you book the flight.

> Timetables, routes, fares and last-service times change, and differ by terminal.
> Confirm on the airport's official site close to your travel date.
