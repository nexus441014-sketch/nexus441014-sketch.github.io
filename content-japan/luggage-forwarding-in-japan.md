## What it is

A door-to-door courier service, commonly called takkyubin, that will take your suitcase
from one hotel and deliver it to another, or to an airport. You hand it over at a hotel
front desk or a convenience store, and collect it at the other end.

It is ordinary, reliable, and used constantly by Japanese travellers. It is also almost
invisible in English-language trip planning.

## Why it changes a trip

Japanese stations are built for people with hands free. Long transfer corridors, stairs
where you expect escalators, crowded trains, and luggage racks sized for a modest bag.
A large suitcase turns a simple connection into a physical ordeal, and makes you
reluctant to stop anywhere on the way.

Sending the bag ahead means:

- **You can sightsee on a transfer day.** Leave Kyoto in the morning, spend the afternoon
  somewhere between, arrive in Tokyo in the evening and the suitcase is already there.
- **Rush hour stops mattering.** A day bag on a packed train is fine. A suitcase is not.
- **The fastest services get easier.** Oversized luggage on some express trains has its
  own rules and may require a particular seat booked in advance. Not carrying it removes
  the problem.

## How it works, step by step

1. Ask at your hotel's front desk, usually the morning you want to send it. Many hotels
   handle this as a matter of course, including filling in the form with you.
2. Give the destination: the next hotel's name, address and phone number, and the date
   you want it to arrive. Have this written down or on your phone.
3. Pay, and keep the tracking slip.
4. Collect it at the next hotel's front desk.

Convenience stores also accept parcels if your accommodation does not offer the service.

## The timing rule that catches people

**Delivery usually takes a day.** Longer for distant regions, islands, or over busy
periods. It is not a same-day service between cities.

So the pattern that works is: send the bag the morning you leave, and have it arrive at
the next hotel the following day, with an overnight bag carried for the night in between.
People who send a bag expecting it to beat them there the same afternoon are the ones who
have a bad experience.

Tell your receiving hotel that a bag is coming, especially if it will arrive before you
check in. Most will hold it without any fuss, but it is a courtesy that avoids confusion.

## Airport delivery, and when not to use it

You can send luggage to a departure airport, or from an arrival airport to your first
hotel, which makes the trip in from the airport considerably more pleasant.

One caution: if you are claiming a **tax refund on shopping**, the goods may need to be
with you at departure for the customs check. Sending them ahead can forfeit the refund.
See [tax-free shopping in Japan](/articles/tax-free-shopping-in-japan.html) and decide
which matters more before you ship.

## What not to send

Anything you cannot be without for a day: medication, travel documents, electronics you
need, chargers, a change of clothes. The suitcase is for the things you will want
tomorrow, not tonight.

## Cost

Priced by size and distance, and modest relative to what it saves. We do not quote
figures here because they vary by carrier, route and season; the counter or front desk
will tell you before you commit.
