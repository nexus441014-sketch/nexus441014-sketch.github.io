7 Free AI Productivity Tools for Solo Business Owners

Running a one-person business means switching between writing, research, planning, customer communication, and administration. AI can help with parts of that workload—but you do not need seven subscriptions to start.

Quick picks

These are seven tools with free access or free-tier options worth evaluating. Availability, usage limits, and pricing vary by region and may change.

1. ChatGPT — brainstorming and drafting

Use ChatGPT to outline emails, generate checklists, and turn messy notes into structured drafts. Review any factual claims and avoid entering confidential client information.

Official website

2. Claude — long-form writing and review

Claude can help revise long documents, improve clarity, and compare alternative approaches. Test the free offering before paying for higher limits.

Official website

3. Google Gemini — idea development

Try Gemini for brainstorming, summarizing your own notes, and generating task breakdowns. Verify outputs before using them for clients.

Official website

4. Perplexity — research starting points

Perplexity can help locate sources quickly. Open and evaluate the original sources: an AI-generated summary is not a substitute for verification.

Official website

5. Canva — simple marketing visuals

Canva offers design tools and templates for social graphics and presentations. Some AI features, assets, and exports may require payment.

Official website

6. Notion — notes and project organization

Notion is useful for tracking clients, tasks, and reusable processes. Its AI capabilities may have separate limits or charges, so check current plan details.

Official website

7. Grammarly — editing business writing

Grammarly can help catch language issues in customer-facing writing. Free features differ from paid writing and AI assistance.

Official website

A simple workflow you can try today

  1. Write down a recurring task that takes at least 20 minutes.
  2. Use one AI assistant to draft a checklist or first version.
  3. Check facts, tone, and any customer-specific details yourself.
  4. Save the approved result as a reusable template.
  5. Measure the time saved across three attempts before subscribing.

Which tool should you start with?

Start with one general-purpose assistant for drafting and planning. Add a research tool only when you need sources, and a design tool only when you regularly create graphics. The best stack is the smallest one that reliably saves you time.

Before you upgrade

Check usage caps, export options, privacy policies, commercial-use terms, and cancellation rules on the provider's official website. Do not assume a feature remains free because a blog post says so.
