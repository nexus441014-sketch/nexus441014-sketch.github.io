7 Free AI Productivity Tools for Solo Business Owners

Running a one-person business means switching between writing, research, planning, customer communication, and administration. AI can help with parts of that workload—but you do not need seven subscriptions to start.

Quick picks

These are seven tools that offer free access or a free tier worth evaluating. Availability, usage limits, and pricing vary by region and change frequently, so treat this as a shortlist to test rather than a specification sheet.

1. ChatGPT — brainstorming and drafting

Use it to outline emails, generate checklists, and turn messy notes into structured drafts. Review any factual claim it produces, and avoid pasting confidential client information into any assistant you have not reviewed the privacy terms for.

Official website

2. Claude — long-form writing and review

Useful when you need to revise a long document, tighten unclear writing, or compare two approaches to the same problem. Test the free offering on a real task before paying for higher limits.

Official website

3. Google Gemini — idea development

Reasonable for brainstorming, summarizing your own notes, and breaking a vague goal into tasks. Verify anything you plan to send to a client.

Official website

4. Perplexity — research starting points

Helps you locate sources quickly. Open the original sources and judge them yourself: an AI summary is a starting point, not verification.

Official website

5. Canva — simple marketing visuals

Design tools and templates for social graphics and simple presentations. Some AI features, stock assets, and export formats may require a paid plan.

Official website

6. Notion — notes and project organization

Useful for tracking clients, tasks, and reusable processes in one place. AI capabilities are typically billed or limited separately from the core workspace, so check the current plan details.

Official website

7. Grammarly — editing business writing

Catches language issues in customer-facing writing. The free feature set differs from the paid writing and AI assistance.

Official website

A simple workflow you can try today

  1. Write down a recurring task that takes you at least 20 minutes.
  2. Use one AI assistant to draft a checklist or a first version.
  3. Check facts, tone, and any customer-specific details yourself.
  4. Save the approved result as a reusable template.
  5. Measure the time saved across three attempts before subscribing to anything.

Which tool should you start with?

Start with one general-purpose assistant for drafting and planning. Add a research tool only when you regularly need sources, and a design tool only when you regularly produce graphics. The best stack is the smallest one that reliably saves you time.

Before you upgrade

Check usage caps, export options, the privacy policy, commercial-use terms, and cancellation rules on the provider's official website. Do not assume a feature is still free because a blog post said so—including this one.
