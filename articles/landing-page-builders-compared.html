Landing Page Builders for Small Businesses: How to Choose

You probably need one page that converts, not a website. That changes the decision completely—and often means you should not buy a page builder at all.

Start by checking whether you need one

Three situations, three different answers:

You already have a website you can edit. Add a page to it. A dedicated builder adds a subdomain, a second bill, and a second place where your content lives. Skip it.

You have no site and no list. A builder bundled with your email platform is usually the best value, because the form, the page, and the follow-up email are in one account with one set of contacts.

You run paid traffic and need to test variants. Here a specialist builder earns its price, because A/B testing, speed, and conversion tooling are the product rather than an add-on.

The four things that actually matter

Speed. A slow landing page loses visitors before they read the headline, and it costs you in search visibility. Test any candidate's own template with a page-speed tool before committing.

Form to follow-up path. A submitted form must land somewhere useful—your email platform, with a tag, triggering a welcome email. If that requires a paid connector on top, count that in the price.

Mobile rendering. Most of your traffic is mobile. Build your test page and look at it on a phone, not in the builder's mobile preview.

Export and portability. Can you take the page, or at least the copy and the contacts, with you? Pages built in a proprietary builder generally cannot move, which is an argument for keeping the page simple.

What does not matter as much as the marketing suggests

  • Template count. You will use one.
  • Animation and interaction. It rarely increases conversions and often slows the page.
  • Built-in analytics dashboards. Your email platform and a simple analytics tool cover what you need.
  • AI copy generation. Useful, but not a reason to choose a platform—you can draft copy anywhere.

The 30-minute test

  1. Build the same page in two candidates: headline, three bullets, one form, one button.
  2. Publish both and open them on your phone over mobile data.
  3. Run each URL through a page-speed tool.
  4. Submit the form on both and confirm the contact arrives in your email platform with the right tag.
  5. Try to change the headline and republish. Count the clicks.
  6. Pick the faster, simpler one.

The page itself matters more than the builder

One headline that names who the page is for. Three bullets about the outcome, not the features. One form with as few fields as you can accept—usually email alone. One button with a verb on it. Social proof if you genuinely have it, and nothing if you do not.

A plain fast page with clear copy outperforms a beautiful slow page with vague copy, on every platform in the table above.
