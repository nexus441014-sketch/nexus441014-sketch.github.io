9 Things First-Time Visitors to Japan Get Wrong

These are not etiquette gotchas. They are the planning and logistics mistakes that actually cost people days, money and temper on a first trip.

1. Packing the itinerary too tightly

Japan rewards depth over coverage. Three cities in ten days is comfortable. Six is a blur of stations. Transfer days consume more hours than the timetable suggests once you include getting to the station, luggage, and finding your accommodation at the other end.

Cut one city. You will see more.

2. Buying a rail pass without doing the arithmetic

The pass is heavily promoted because resellers earn on it. For a lot of first itineraries it costs more than the individual tickets. Price your actual route first: see do you need a Japan Rail Pass.

3. Dragging a large suitcase everywhere

Stations are built for people with free hands. You can send the case ahead to your next hotel for a modest fee and travel with a day bag, which is what residents do. See luggage forwarding.

4. Assuming cards work everywhere, or that nothing takes cards

Both halves of the reputation are wrong. Chains, hotels and transport take cards; small independent restaurants, shrines, rural buses and festival stalls often do not. Carry both: cash or card in Japan.

5. Planning around the old tax-free system

The rules changed on 1 November 2026. You now pay full price at the register and claim the refund at departure, which means you need more money on the day and an extra airport step. Older guides, and plenty of current ones, still describe the previous system. See tax-free shopping in Japan.

6. Sorting out connectivity after arriving

Every other decision assumes you are online. Buy the eSIM or book the router before you fly, and check your phone is unlocked and compatible while you can still do something about it: eSIM, pocket WiFi or SIM.

7. Underestimating how early things close

Last trains are earlier than visitors expect, and missing one in a large city is an expensive taxi. Many restaurants outside nightlife districts stop seating well before people assume, and in smaller towns the gap between lunch and dinner service catches travellers out. Check the last connection before you commit to an evening on the other side of the city.

8. Travelling during the big domestic holiday weeks without planning

There are periods when the entire country travels at once. Trains fill, accommodation prices rise, and popular sights become genuinely unpleasant. If your dates are fixed and fall inside one, book transport and rooms much further ahead than you otherwise would. Check the calendar before setting dates if you have the freedom.

9. Not setting up the arrival procedures in advance

Immigration and customs steps can be prepared online before you fly through the government's Visit Japan Web service. Doing it on the plane with no connectivity, or in the queue, is a worse experience than ten minutes at home.

The underlying pattern

Almost every item on this list is the same mistake: deciding something at the moment it becomes urgent, rather than before departure, when you had time and internet. Connectivity, airport transfer, rail pass arithmetic, cash, arrival paperwork. An hour of planning removes most of what goes wrong on a first trip.
