Japan Travel Apps Worth Installing

Most "best apps for Japan" lists are twenty apps long. You need about five, and three of them you already have. The value is in the setup, not the download.

Do the setup before you fly

Every item below works better if you configure it on home wifi. Airport wifi is fine but you will be tired, and some of these downloads are large.

The single highest-value ten minutes of trip preparation: download the offline translation pack, download the offline map of your cities, and add a transport card to your phone wallet if your phone supports it.

1. A maps app, with transit

Google Maps handles Japanese public transport unusually well: platform numbers, which carriage to board, exit numbers, and fares. For most visitors it is the only navigation tool needed.

Setup worth doing:

  • Download offline maps for each city you will visit
  • Save your accommodation as a pin before you leave, with the address in Japanese
  • Learn to read the exit number it gives you — in Japan that matters more than the

street name

Its one weakness is rail pass validity: it will route you onto trains a pass excludes.

2. A route planner that understands passes

If you hold a Japan Rail Pass or a regional pass, you need something that filters by validity. Japan Transit Planner and Jorudan both do this; so does the operator's own app.

This is only worth installing if you actually bought a pass. Otherwise skip it — see do you need a Japan Rail Pass and riding trains in Japan.

3. A translation app, with the camera

Google Translate or your platform's equivalent. The camera mode is the feature that matters — pointed at a menu, a medicine box, a sign or a label.

Two setup steps, both essential:

  1. Download Japanese for offline use. Basements, some restaurants and rural areas have

no usable signal.

  1. Practise the camera mode once at home so you are not learning it in front of a queue.

Do you need to speak Japanese covers where this does and does not close the gap.

4. Your phone's wallet, with a transport card

If your phone supports it, adding Suica or Pasmo to your phone wallet is the single biggest convenience upgrade available. You tap your phone at the gate, top up from your phone, and never queue at a machine.

The catch is device compatibility, which varies by phone model and region of purchase. If it works for you, it replaces a physical card entirely; if not, a physical card is still easy to get. IC cards explained covers both routes.

5. A disaster alert app

Safety tips is the official multilingual alert app for visitors, covering earthquake early warnings, tsunami advisories, weather warnings and evacuation information.

An earthquake early warning gives you seconds, not minutes — but seconds are enough to step away from a window or stop pouring boiling water. See is Japan safe for what to do when one fires.

Apps you can skip

  • Phrasebook apps. The translation camera has replaced them.
  • Currency converters. Your phone's search bar does this.
  • Individual city guide apps. Heavy, out of date, and duplicate your maps app.
  • Wifi-finder apps. With an eSIM you will not need them, and without one they are

unreliable.

Connectivity is the dependency

Every app above except the offline packs assumes you have data. That decision — eSIM, pocket wifi or a physical SIM — is the one to settle before any of this matters, and it is covered in eSIM, pocket wifi or SIM.

The ten-minute checklist

Before you leave home wifi:

  1. Offline maps downloaded for each city
  2. Japanese downloaded in the translation app
  3. Transport card added to your phone wallet, or a plan to buy a physical one
  4. Alert app installed and notifications allowed
  5. Accommodation addresses saved, in Japanese
  6. A screenshot of each booking confirmation, so a dead signal cannot strand you

That last one is not an app, and it has saved more trips than any of them.

Official sources

  • Safety tips app (JNTO) — the official multilingual disaster alert app for visitors
  • JR East — Suica — IC card information, including mobile use
  • Japan National Tourism Organization — planning — official travel information
App features, device compatibility and coverage change. Confirm on the provider's own page, and check mobile IC card support against your specific phone model before relying on it.
