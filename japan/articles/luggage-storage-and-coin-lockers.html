Luggage Storage and Coin Lockers in Japan

The gap between an 11am checkout and a 3pm check-in is the most predictable problem of a Japan trip. There are four ways to solve it, and the obvious one fails most often.

The problem is a four-hour gap

Japanese hotels usually check out around 10 or 11 and check in around 3. On a day you change cities, you have bags and no room at both ends. Travelling with them is possible but miserable on a crowded train.

Four solutions, roughly in order of how often they work:

  1. Leave them at the hotel — free, and the first thing to try
  2. Station coin lockers — fast, but full at the worst times
  3. A staffed storage counter or a storage app — reliable, costs more
  4. Forward them ahead — best for longer gaps, needs a day

Hotels hold bags, before and after

Almost every Japanese hotel will store your luggage on your checkout day, and most will hold it on your arrival day before check-in. This is normal and usually free.

Ask at the desk rather than assuming. The phrase is simple enough to mime, and the desk will give you a tag. Two limits to know:

  • Some smaller hotels and guesthouses will not hold bags after checkout day
  • Very few will hold them overnight between two separate stays

If your route brings you back to the same city, ask whether they will hold a bag for the days in between. Many will, and it saves carrying a case around a mountain town.

Coin lockers: how they actually work

Station lockers come in roughly three sizes: small (daypack), medium (cabin bag), large (checked suitcase). Payment is by coins or, at newer banks of lockers, by IC card.

Two traps worth naming:

  • IC card lockers open with the card you paid with. If you return a physical card or

switch phones, you cannot retrieve your bag without staff help.

  • A new calendar day means a new charge, payable before the door opens.

Why the lockers are full

Large lockers at major stations are routinely all taken during the middle of the day, especially at Kyoto, Shinjuku, Tokyo, Osaka and Shin-Osaka, and especially in peak seasons.

What actually works:

  • Go early. Before 9am you will find space; by 11am often not.
  • Walk away from the main concourse. Lockers near the shinkansen gates fill first;

banks near minor exits, on other floors, or in the connected shopping complex are emptier.

  • Try the next station along. One stop out, the problem usually disappears.
  • Use two medium lockers instead of hunting for one large one.

Some large stations have a locker availability screen or app showing free units by bank — worth looking for before walking the building.

Staffed storage and storage apps

When lockers fail, these are the fallback:

  • Station luggage offices at major stations take oversized items and charge per bag per

day. Slower than a locker, but they do not run out in the same way.

  • Storage-sharing services let you reserve space in a nearby shop, café or hotel

through an app, in advance. The reservation is the point: you know before you set out that your bags have a home.

Both cost more than a locker. On a day where the alternative is dragging a suitcase around Kyoto in August, they are worth it.

When to forward instead

If the gap is longer than a day, or you are going somewhere hard to reach with a case — a hot spring town, a hike, two nights with one small bag — forwarding is the better answer. It is cheap, extremely reliable, and typically next-day. Luggage forwarding in Japan covers how to send it and what the timing means for your packing.

The rule of thumb: under a day, store it; over a day, send it.

Shinkansen luggage, briefly

On some shinkansen routes, oversized cases now need a reserved space booked with the seat. If your suitcase is at the large end, this changes your booking rather than your storage — see riding trains in Japan.

The practical checklist

For any city-change day:

  1. Leave the bags at the hotel in the morning if you are coming back past it
  2. If not, head to the station before 9am and take a locker then
  3. Keep ¥100 coins and a ¥500 coin on you specifically for this
  4. Photograph the locker number and the bank — they look identical and stations are

large

  1. If you paid by IC card, do not change cards before you come back
  2. If nothing is free, move one floor or one station before paying for staffed storage

Done in that order, the four-hour gap stops being a thing you plan your day around.
