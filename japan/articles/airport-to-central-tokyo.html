Getting from Narita or Haneda into Central Tokyo

Your first hour in Japan is the one where you are most tired, most loaded with luggage, and least able to work out a transport map. Decide this before you fly.

First, which airport

Haneda is close to central Tokyo. Narita is a long way out, in a different prefecture. If you have a choice when booking, this difference is worth real money and about an hour of your life in each direction.

If you have already booked, the decision below is about which service from your airport.

The four factors

  1. What time do you land? Late arrivals lose options fast. Rail and bus services stop

earlier than visitors expect, and the last connection from Narita is not late.

  1. How much luggage? A large case changes the calculation towards a bus or a taxi,

because they are door-to-door and you are not lifting anything onto a train.

  1. Where are you staying? A hotel near a major station favours the train. Somewhere

residential favours the bus or a taxi for the last stretch.

  1. How many of you? For three or four people, a taxi stops looking extravagant

compared with four separate train fares, particularly late at night.

The options in plain terms

Express trains are the fastest from Narita and reliable from both airports. They run on a fixed schedule, with reserved seating on some services and luggage space designed for airport use. The weakness is that they deliver you to a major station, not to your hotel, so you still have a last leg with your bags.

Local and metro lines are the cheapest. They are also the most tiring: more changes, more stairs, and carriages that are not built for suitcases. Reasonable from Haneda, punishing from Narita after a long flight.

Airport buses run to major hotels and districts. Slower in traffic, but you sit down, your luggage goes in the hold, and many routes stop at or near hotels. For a first arrival with a big case, this is frequently the right answer even though nobody recommends it online.

Taxis are expensive from Narita and reasonable from Haneda, especially shared between several people or very late at night when little else is running.

Set these up before you leave the airport

  • Get online. Sort connectivity before you walk away from the arrivals hall. See

eSIM, pocket WiFi or SIM.

  • Get an IC card. It makes every subsequent journey trivial. See

IC cards explained.

  • Consider sending the suitcase. You can forward it to your hotel and travel in with

a day bag. See luggage forwarding.

  • Have your hotel's address in Japanese saved on your phone, and the phone number.

This matters for taxis and for luggage forwarding.

The late arrival problem

If you land in the evening, check the last departure for your intended service before you fly, not while standing in the arrivals hall. Options thin out quickly, and the fallback from Narita is an expensive taxi or an airport hotel.

Both airports publish current access information and timetables:

  • Narita Airport — official access guide
  • Haneda Airport — official access guide

The default recommendations

  • Haneda, any time, light luggage: train. It is quick and simple.
  • Haneda, late or heavy: taxi, especially shared.
  • Narita, daytime, light luggage: express train.
  • Narita, heavy luggage or a hotel away from a main station: airport bus.
  • Narita, late arrival: check the last services before you book the flight.
Timetables, routes, fares and last-service times change, and differ by terminal. Confirm on the airport's official site close to your travel date.
