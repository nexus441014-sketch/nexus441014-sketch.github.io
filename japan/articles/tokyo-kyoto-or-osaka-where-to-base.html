Tokyo, Kyoto or Osaka: Where to Base Yourself

Kyoto and Osaka are close enough that choosing both as bases wastes a day. Tokyo is far enough that skipping it changes the trip. Here is how the three actually compare as places to put your suitcase.

The decision is really two decisions

One: how many nights in Tokyo. It is a long way from the other two and has more inside it than either. This is a real choice.

Two: Kyoto or Osaka — not both. They are around half an hour apart by train. Sleeping in one and visiting the other is normal. Sleeping in both costs you a packing day for almost no travel saved.

Almost every "Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka" itinerary should be a two-base trip, not three.

Choose Kyoto if the temples are the point

Kyoto rewards being there early and late. The famous places are genuinely different at 7am, and you can only do that reliably if you sleep nearby — Kyoto without the crowds is mostly about this.

The trade-offs are real: rooms cost more in spring and autumn, the bus network does a lot of the work the subway does elsewhere, and dinner options thin out earlier than you expect.

Choose Osaka if the evenings and the budget are the point

Osaka gives you better rooms for the money, a stronger late-night food scene, and almost the same access westward. Kyoto becomes a day trip, which costs you the early mornings but nothing else.

It is also the better hub if you are flying out of Kansai, or heading to Hiroshima, Kobe or Shikoku afterwards.

The honest case for sleeping in both

There are two:

  1. Long trips. Over two weeks, a move costs you proportionally less.
  2. Kyoto in peak season. If Kyoto rooms are priced badly for your dates, splitting the

stay can be cheaper than absorbing the peak rate for a whole week.

Otherwise, pick one and use the train.

Tokyo: how many nights

Tokyo is not a city you finish. The useful question is how many districts you want, because that is what you actually consume.

  • Three nights — two or three districts, no day trips
  • Four to five — enough for a day trip and still a normal pace
  • A week — you start seeing the ordinary city rather than the listed one

Where you sleep inside Tokyo matters more than in the other two. A station on the Yamanote loop or a well-connected subway interchange turns a 50-minute crossing into 25. Where to stay in Japan covers the accommodation types themselves — business hotel, ryokan, guesthouse, apartment.

Which order?

Fly into Tokyo and out of Osaka (or the reverse) if your ticket allows it. An open-jaw flight saves you a full backtrack day, and the two airports are served by the same long-haul routes.

If you must return to the same airport, do Tokyo last. Ending in the city with the most options, the best transport to the airport, and the widest choice of last-minute dinner is easier than ending in Kyoto and having to cross the country tired.

Booking the rooms

Whichever you pick, the thing that bites is not price — it is availability in late March to early April, and again in late October to November. Those are the two windows where good rooms in Kyoto go months out.

For the same room appearing on several sites, compare before you commit. Klook's hotel listings cover all three cities and show the cancellation terms on the listing, which is the detail that matters most when your route is not final yet.

Hold the dates you are certain about, leave the slow middle of the trip until later, and you can still change your mind about Kyoto versus Osaka a month out.
