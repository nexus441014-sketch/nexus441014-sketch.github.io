Affiliate Disclosure

Current status

At the time of writing, this site contains no affiliate links and earns nothing. Every link in every guide points to an operator, airport, government body or company because it is the right source for that step, not because of any commercial arrangement.

If that changes

Japan Explained may join affiliate programmes for services it already recommends, such as connectivity, rail passes, accommodation or activity booking. If it does:

  • Affiliate links will be clearly labelled, and a disclosure will appear in the article

near the link, above the point where you would click it.

  • This page will be updated to name the programmes before any link goes live.
  • A commission costs you nothing extra. The price you pay is the same.

What will not change

The commercial side will not be allowed to decide the recommendation. Specifically:

  • A service will not be recommended because it pays more than the alternative. Where a

cheaper or free option is better for most readers, the guide will say so, including "you probably do not need this".

  • The official, non-commission option will still be linked where one exists. The railway

operator's own page beats a reseller for most people, and the guides say that.

  • Paid placements, if ever accepted, will be labelled as advertising and kept separate

from the guides.

  • No guide will claim first-hand testing of a service that has not been used.

Why this matters more for Japan

A lot of English-language Japan content pushes products that visitors do not need — rail passes that cost more than the tickets they replace, data plans sized for nobody. Those recommendations usually exist because of the commission. The point of this site is to tell you when the answer is "skip it", and that only works if the money is visible.
